# Briefing

---
# Ground 
- MACTR (Tower) is responsible for all ground movements.
- Single apron with two entry and exit taxiways. Typical operation is one taxiway is used for arrivals, the other for departures.
## Departures
- Departures must backtrack to full runway length.
- Maximum of two simultaneous pushbacks in the apron at any time, to avoid apron congestion.
- Due to ground layout, ensure pushback clearance sequence respects departure sequence. Once  pushback is initiated it may be impossible to modify the departure sequence, as there are very limited overtaking options while taxiing.
- Stands A18, A19, and any other aircraft parked nose towards the island taxi straight out without pushing back.
- Powerback type maneuvers are allowed.
## Arrivals
- Apron entrance taxiway must be included in taxi instructions. Entry will typically be via the taxiway away from the active runway threshold.
!!!info Note
Traffic permitting, consider instructing via the other taxiway in cases of apron congestion. Example: Runway 05, departure pushing back from A12, arrival inbound stand A05. Non-standard apron entry via taxiway C might avoid a delay in case the departure is still in the apron.
## Stand Assignment
- Stands A14 through A17 cannot be used simultaneously with stand A18.
- Stands A1 through A4 cannot be used simultaneously with stand A19.
- Stand A14 cannot be used simultaneously with stand A15.
- Stand A16 cannot be used simultaneously with stand A17
-  Stands A12, A14 and A18 cannot be used simultaneously with stand A17 if the aircraft at A13 has a larger wingspan than 45m (B763)
- Small General Aviation aircraft may be instructed to taxi to stand A01, despite parking beyond it and facing to the island
## Engine run-ups
Engine test runs may only take place:
- Runway or Runway Holding Points.
- Short Engine checks at Idle Power are allowed on stand.
## Phraseology
### Pushback
- "[Callsign], push and start approved, [runway in use]."
### Taxi out
- "[Callsign], taxi, backtrack and line up [runway in use]."
OR
- "[Callsign], taxi to holding point [runway] via [taxiway], hold short of runway."
### Taxi out with powerback
- "[Callsign] powerback, taxi, backtrack and line up [runway in use]."
OR
- "[Callsign] taxi to holding point [runway] via [taxiway], hold short of runway."
### Taxi in
- "[Callsign] backtrack [runway in use], vacate via [exit taxiway], [stand]."
OR
- "[Callsign] vacate via [exit taxiway], [stand]."

# Tower
- High and fast rising terrain northwest of the runway. Most activity will be over the shoreline or over water.
- **Four** different wind sensors are available: RosÃ¡rio, Threshold runway 05, Midpoint and Threshold runway 23. Alternatively, use IPMA nsWEBPIP. No account is required, access via Data Query/Metar.
## Area of Responsibility
- MACTR is responsible for runway 05/23, the taxiways, and the CTR airspace as defined in AIP. Vertical limits are defined as surface up to 2000ft AMSL.
- Porto da Cruz and Funchal city are good visual references of the limits of the CTR.
## Departures
Departures from runway 05 may line up while an arrival is still in the approach. Take off clearance may be issued after the arrival passes abeam the threshold of runway 23. **This means that once the landing aircraft has touched down and safely rolled past the far end (abeam the Runway 23 threshold), it is considered past the point of any potential conflict with a departure. ATC can then immediately issue a takeoff clearance to the next aircraft lined up on Runway 05, allowing it to depart in the same direction before the landed aircraft even fully vacates via a taxiway at the far end**.

Wind to be given on takeoff clearance:
- RWY 05: Midpoint (METAR wind)
- RWY 23: Midpoint (METAR wind) and Rosario

If there are other aircraft under control of MACTR, with which Visual Separation in the Vicinity of
Aerodrome will be applied, instruct departures to remain on Tower frequency after departure. Hand off to MATMA shall only occur after radar separation exists between the departure and all other
traffic under control of MACTR
## Arrivals
It is recommended to request to report when turning base to runway 05.

Wind to be given on landing clearance:
- RWY 05: Touchdown 05 and Rosario
- RWY 23: Touchdown 23

Traffic may need to backtrack, especially on runway 05 or Heavy WTC aircraft. As soon as it becomes obvious that the arrival has touched down and a go around is unlikely to
happen, inform MATMA that the arrival is on the ground.
## Go arounds
Go around procedure is different depending on the approach being flown:
- For **RNP** Approach 05/23 -> Proceed to **MONEC**, 3000ft.
- For **VOR DME** Approach 05/23 - Proceed to **FUSUL**, 3000ft.

For runway 23, give Rosario wind as soon as possible.

Runway 23 go around profile conflicts with the departure profile.

Inform MATMA and transfer control and communications.
## VFR Traffic
VFR traffic **must** exit the CTR in the presence of IFR approaches, as there is nowhere within the CTR where it can orbit without interfering with the instrument approaches.
## Phraseology
### Winds
**Calm wind (<=1kt)**:
- "[Callsign], wind calm, [rest of information]."
**Variable direction (e.g. VRB03KT)**:
- "[Callsign], wind variable 3 knots, [rest of information]."
**Directional variation (e.g. 02010KT 350V070)**:
- "[Callsign], wind 020 degrees, 10 knots, variable between 350 and 070 degrees, [rest of information]."
**Gusty winds (e.g. 02010G20KT)**:
- "[Callsign], wind 020 degrees, 10 knots, maximum 20 knots, [rest of information]."
**Both directional and gust winds (e.g. 02010G20KT 350V070)**:
- "[Callsign], wind 020 degrees, 10 knots, maximum 20 knots, variable between 350 and 070 degrees, [rest of information]."
### Take off
Runway 05:
- "[Callsign] wind at midpoint [wind], runway 05, cleared for take off."
Runway 23: 
- "[Callsign] wind at midpoint [wind], rosario [wind], runway 23, cleared for take off."
### Landing
Runway 05:
- "[Callsign] wind rosario [wind], touchdown [wind], runway 05, cleared to land."
Runway 23:
- "[Callsign] wind touchdown [wind], runway 23, cleared to land."
### Traffic Information
You must provide pilots with relevant traffic information to help mantain safe separation. Examples could be:
- VFR traffic's converging.
- IFR departure or arrival with a VFR in the circuit.

To give traffic information use the following format:
- "[Callsign], traffic information, [type of aircraft], [number] O'clock, [distance], [altitude/level]."

**Common Example**: "CSAUD, traffic information, Cessna 172, 2 O'clock, 4 miles, 1500ft."
### Aircraft Identification
As Madeira Tower is radar-based for primary air traffic control, identification is only used on first contact with an airborne VFR aircraft.
- "[Callsign], Madeira Tower, identified."

If the VFR is entering the CTR from another aerodrome OR comes back after a long time, the ATIS code and local QNH must be given:
- "[Callsign], Madeira Tower, identified, current information [code], QNH [QNH]."
### Go Around
- "[Callsign] go around, [reason]."

**Here, further instructions must be given once the aircraft is on a stable climb.**
### Rejected Take Off
If the aircraft has not begun the takeoff roll:
- "[Callsign] HOLD POSITION, CANCEL TAKE OFF I SAY AGAIN CANCEL TAKE OFF."

If the aircraft has just began the takeoff roll:
- "[Callsign] STOP IMMEDIATELY, [Callsign] STOP IMMEDIATELY."
## Emergencies
An emergency will likely impact tower operations, as it often results in the runway being unavailable for normal operations for a period of time.

First and foremost, any aircraft known or suspected to be in a state of emergency must be given priority over all other aircraft.

Best practices in handling emergencies can be summarized in the **ASSIST** principle (**A** - Acknowledge; **S** - Separate, **S** - Silence; **I** - Inform, **S** - Support, **T** - Time):
- **A**cknowledge the call
- **S**eparate the aircraft from other traffic. Give it room to manoeuvre
- **S**ilence on the frequency. Provide separate frequency where possible - this prevents unnecessary clutter for the pilots
- **I**nform adjacent controllers
- **S**upport the pilots in any way possible - Start to think of alternative routings, etc.
- **T**ime - Give the pilots time to collect their thoughts, don't harass them for information. Time produces good decisions
## Extra
When you want to let a VFR continue via a filed route:
- "[Callsign], continue via route and profile."

When you want to let a VFR continue as you cleared them:
- "[Callsign], continue as cleared."

# Approach
Madeira TMA, MATMA, is vertically delimited from FL115 down to 1000FT AGL.
## Departures
Departures climb to FL240, or cruise level if lower.

**A release from MATMA is required for all departures**:
- Release may be given to MACTR for departure if no arrival has crossed past their IAF and no aircraft is holding at FUSUL below FL70. In addition, GALOZ and NIDUL departures must not have any aircraft holding at FUSUL below FL110.
## SID Deviations/Directs
Any deviation from procedures must be above ?[MRVA](lowest altitude, measured in feet above mean sea level, that an air traffic control radar controller can assign to an aircraft while guiding it by radar vectoring. Check LPMA ATC Surveillance Minimum Altitude Chart) in IMC. Visual departures are available on request from the pilots if they are able to maintain own separation with the terrain.

When clear of conflicts inside the TMA, coordinate direct to FIR exit point, IAF or first point of STAR
with enroute sector. 
## Arrivals
### Initial Contact
Report current ATIS. Current delay expected may also be transmitted if relevant.
### Delays
MATMA shall determine an expected approach time for an arriving aircraft that will be subjected to a delay of 10 minutes or more.

Calculation is done manually, by taking the estimated time over the IAF of the first arrival, and then assigning EATs separated by:
- 7 minutes for LPMA runway 05.
- 4 minutes for LPMA runway 23.

ABUSU and PILIM holding can be used simultaneously and shall be considered the same volume.
### Non-RNAV
Enroute sector will initiate an Approval Request for Non-RNAV arrivals. Situation permitting, should be cleared DCT FUN, SNT or ABUSU, then vectored to a VOR approach.
### Approach Sequence
Target arrival sequence separation of 7 minutes for Runway 05, 10NM for Runway 23, measured at IAF.
### Approach Sector
All arrivals may proceed directly to approach IAF when convenient. NIDUL arrivals are recommended to remain in their STAR, to allow for a better descent profile.

Traffic permitting, all arrivals, except NIDUL, may descend to 3000FT. NIDUL arrivals should descend to FL90 until MA542, then to 3000FT.

- RNP A, RNP Z, VOR DME 05: Approach clearance may only be issued after Madeira Tower confirms the previous arrival is on the ground, due to conflicting with Missed Approach path.
- RNP Y, RNP 23 via MONEC: Do not descend below 4000FT before Madeira Tower confirms the previous arrival is on the ground. Approach clearance may only be issued thereafter, due to conflicting with Missed Approach path.

Avoid the use of holding at Missed Approach Holding Point, to prevent loss of separation between two consecutive approaches
### Speed Restrictions
Descending and arriving aircraft may be instructed to reduce to a speed not lower than:
- Below FL250 -> 250kts
- Below FL150 -> 220kts / minimum clean speed
- Below FL70 -> 180kts
- Below 4000ft/FAP -> 160kts / minimum approach speed
## Phraseology
### IFR Approach
- "[Callsign] cleared [approach], report visual."
(After pilot reports visual)
- "[Callsign] cleared Visual Approach [runway], contact tower, 124.660."
### Visual Departure
- "[Callsign] proceed direct [point], maintain own separation with the terrain."
### Aircraft Identification
- "[Callsign], Madeira Approach, identified."
